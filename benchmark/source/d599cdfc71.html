06  /  ABOUT THE CONSERVANCY

Ridgeline Trail Conservancy was established in 1987 by a coalition of backcountry users, land managers, and municipal planners who recognized that the trail network crossing the Northern Territory Management Area had no formal stewardship organization. What began with twelve volunteers and a single maintained corridor has expanded to cover 47.3 miles of trail across 12,400 acres of managed land.

The Conservancy's field methodology follows the survey documentation standard established in coordination with the National Park Service and adapted for this terrain class. Every trail in the network is assigned a survey reference, a revision history, and a dated field log. Nothing is managed on the basis of memory or informal knowledge — the sheet is the record, and the record must be complete.

Crews operate on a volunteer basis, supported by an annual land stewardship grant from the state Department of Natural Resources and individual donor contributions. The Conservancy employs one full-time Trail Coordinator and two seasonal Field Inspectors during the survey season, which runs from May through October. All field notes, condition surveys, and project records are maintained in the public documentation archive and referenced on this sheet.
